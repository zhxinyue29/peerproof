## 二、项目描述（直接粘贴）

### One-liner

> Attendance you don't have to trust the organizer for.

### Problem

> Free RSVP costs nothing to abandon. Monad Blitz has run in 50+ cities since May 2025 — ask any of
> those organizers how many RSVPs walked through the door, and how much room and catering they had
> already committed to the number on the signup page.
>
> Deposit-forfeiture is the obvious fix and it has been tried twice. Kickback shipped it in 2018 and
> sunset in 2023; Unlock Protocol's "I'm Going" shipped a beta in 2024. **Both settle on the
> organizer's word** — an organizer can mark nobody as present and keep the pot. Kickback's own
> self-audit concedes that event owners "possess extensive authority to designate attendance,
> enabling selective exclusion of disliked participants."

### Solution

> Attendees stake a deposit to register. At the venue they attest to each other by scanning
> short-lived codes, and the contract settles automatically: everyone confirmed present reclaims
> their deposit and splits the deposits forfeited by no-shows. The organizer has no function that
> releases, withholds, or receives a single wei.
>
> Three constraints stack to make an attestation mean presence. The peer code expires in fifteen
> seconds, so a forwarded screenshot is worthless within two epochs. Checking in spends a live
> signature from the beacon displayed at the venue in its own transaction, so the chain — not the
> submitter — records when somebody walked in. And being confirmed requires having submitted an
> attestation yourself, which requires having checked in: every account that settles as present
> came through the door. You cannot be relayed in by a friend.
>
> Arrival and vouching run on separate clocks on purpose. A beacon signature carries no proof of
> when it was read, so the only honest way to date one is to make reading it a transaction — and
> once that is done, nothing later in the evening has to touch the venue again. Requiring a fresh
> beacon on every attestation, as an earlier version did, would put a thirty-second deadline on
> finding and greeting another human.

### Why Monad

> This is not "Monad is faster, so it's nicer." The mechanism does not work on a slow chain.
>
> Fifty people scanning three to five peers each is 150–250 concurrent writes inside two minutes,
> each touching independent storage — parallel execution absorbs that without contention, and at
> ~200k gas against a 150M block limit one block holds roughly 750 attestations. Scanning has to
> confirm in under a second or people re-scan and assume it broke; 300ms blocks deliver that. And
> the codes expire in fifteen seconds, so confirmation must beat expiry with margin.
>
> On a twelve-second chain you would batch attestations off chain — which reintroduces a server
> everybody has to trust, and the entire mechanism collapses. Latency is not a UX detail here; it
> is load-bearing.

### What's on chain

> Deposits, attestations, the presence verdict, settlement, and double-claim prevention. Off chain:
> the event title. That is all — nothing that decides where money goes.

### Prior art and delta

> Kickback (2018, sunset 2023) and Unlock's "I'm Going" (2024, beta) both implemented
> deposit-forfeiture for event attendance, and both put the organizer in the settlement path.
> Unlock's own announcement says forfeited funds could "in the future, even be shared with the
> attendees who did attend." **That is the part nobody built.**

### Limitations

> - Onchain proof of physical location is an open problem. The venue beacon raises the cost of
>   remote collusion from zero to "at least one confederate must physically attend and relay a
>   beacon inside its thirty-second epoch, once per remote account." It does not eliminate it.
>   NFC/UWB attestation is future work.
> - The deposit must be the user's own money. Fiat onramps are out of scope, so V1 targets
>   attendees of IRL crypto events who already hold MON but still resent extensions, seed phrases,
>   and gas prompts. Passkey accounts remove the wallet friction, not the funding requirement.
> - WebAuthn PRF is not universally available — Chrome's desktop authenticator exposes none. The
>   app detects this and falls back to a browser wallet, which then confirms each attestation.
> - Passkey loss means fund loss. No recovery mechanism is implemented.
> - Presence uses a naive threshold rather than the K-core of the attestation graph. The K-core is
>   the formally correct set and is computable in O(V+E), but nobody computes global graph
>   properties on chain. The venue beacon is the real sybil defence; graph analysis would be
>   defence in depth.
> - The demo event's no-shows are test accounts we control. Their deposits are real and are really
>   forfeited; they are simply not strangers.
> - The deployment is on Monad testnet with a 1 MON deposit and k = 2. Testnet MON arrives from a
>   faucet in single digits, so the intended 30 MON stake across six accounts is not obtainable;
>   and k = 2 is the highest threshold three filming devices can satisfy, since N mutual vouchers
>   give everyone N−1 vouches. Both are configuration, not mechanism — the contract takes either.

### Revenue

> A cut of **forfeited** deposits only — never of a stake that returns to somebody who showed up.
> The fee is earned exactly when the mechanism did work, and an attendee's principal is never
> touched. Precedent: Unlock's DAO voted a 1% protocol fee switch onto the same shape of contract.
>
> Deliberately not implemented in V1. A fee recipient is a privileged address, and "no privileged
> parties" is the entire differentiator; adding one to win a line on a slide would be a bad trade.

## 三、Bounty 申报

### Best Mera-Powered UX ($2,500)

> Mera is the entire account layer. An attendee arrives on a link, taps Face ID once, and is
> registered — no seed phrase, no extension, no custody backend, and no gas prompt at any point.
>
> The reason this product needs Mera specifically rather than any embedded wallet: attendance codes
> rotate every fifteen seconds, so the signing key has to work without a prompt. Mera's PRF
> derivation gives a plain secp256k1 key held in a signing session, so after one ceremony every
> subsequent signature is silent. A wallet-based signer would prompt per code and the mechanism
> would be unusable — we shipped that path as the fallback precisely so the contrast is visible.
>
> We also ship an honest capability check: `isUserVerifyingPlatformAuthenticatorAvailable` plus the
> `PRF_UNAVAILABLE` error path, with a wallet fallback behind it, because desktop Chrome's built-in
> authenticator has no PRF at all.

### Mera: One Passkey, Many Keys ($2,500)

> The PRF-derived key never signs a transaction. It signs **attendance codes**: an ECDSA signature
> over (contract, eventId, subject, epoch) that the attendee's phone displays as a QR code, and
> that the contract verifies with `ecrecover` for ~3,000 gas.
>
> That makes the passkey a *credential issuer* rather than a wallet. The code is unforgeable and
> non-transferable in the way that matters: a confederate cannot produce your code without your
> passkey, which means without your phone and your biometric.
>
> Two deliberate consequences. First, `ecrecover` on a PRF-derived secp256k1 key costs a third of
> what the P256 precompile costs at `0x0100` (~3k vs ~6.9k measured), which matters across 150+
> attestations. Second, and more important: signing a P256 assertion with the passkey itself would
> demand a biometric prompt every fifteen seconds. PRF derivation is what makes a rotating
> credential possible at all.

### Best Use of Envio ($1,000)

> ⚠️ 待完成——仓库已推送，还差在 Envio 上连仓库并拿到公开 GraphQL 端点。

> The `/verify` page renders the attestation graph — every edge is one transaction — from an Envio
> HyperIndex deployment over the seven contract events. The handlers re-derive nothing: presence is
> whatever `Confirmed` said, so the index can never disagree with the chain.
>
> The frontend keeps a direct-RPC reader behind it (`web/lib/logs.ts`). That is not indecision about
> Envio; it is a response to two measured constraints. Monad's default public endpoints cap
> `eth_getLogs` at 100 blocks per request, which turns a graph render into twenty-odd serial
> round-trips — the reason to index at all. And Envio's free tier removes inactive deployments after
> 30 days, while the judging window closes 35 days after our submission date. A verification page
> that goes blank mid-judging would be worse than a slower one, so the fallback stays.

**放弃它会损失** $1,000 的 bounty 机会和 GraphQL 订阅。功能上不损失——直连 RPC 读日志那条路已经写好并在跑。

### Best workflow with CRE ($3,000)

> ⚠️ 代码已写完（合约 + workflow + 9 个测试），**并已对着真 SDK `@chainlink/cre-sdk@1.20.1` 编译通过**
> （`cd cre/settle-workflow && npm install && npx tsc --noEmit` 退出码 0）。还差你跑一次
> `cre login` + `simulate`。见 `cre/README.md`。
>
> **更正**：这一节原来写的是「cron 触发器自动调用 `settle()`」。那是错的——CRE 不能直接调用任意合约的
> 任意函数，必须经 KeystoneForwarder 调你实现了 `IReceiver` 的合约的 `onReport`。已按真实机制重写。

> `settle` is permissionless on the escrow, which is what stops the organizer from being able to
> withhold payouts. But permissionless is not automatic: without something firing it, the first
> person to think of it pays the gas, and until then confirmed attendees cannot claim. A cron closes
> that gap without adding anyone who has to be trusted — if the workflow never fires, `settle` is
> exactly as callable by any stranger as it was before.
>
> CRE cannot call `settle` directly, so the path is
> `cron → workflow → DON-signed report → KeystoneForwarder → SettleReceiver.onReport → escrow.settle`.
> `SettleReceiver` is 40 lines and holds no authority: it has no owner and no setters, because
> `settle` is already callable by anyone and an `Ownable` would add a privileged address to a system
> whose entire claim is that it has none.
>
> The detail we are most pleased with is a failure mode. Reports that arrive early, twice, or for an
> unknown event are declined and logged rather than reverted — because Chainlink's interface docs say
> a reverting `onReport` "can be retried with a higher gas limit", so bubbling `WindowOpen` from a
> five-minute cron would retry a call that cannot succeed yet, at rising gas, on every tick. Four
> tests pin that.
>
> Gas is pinned at 150,000 rather than estimated, for the same reason it is pinned everywhere else in
> this project: Monad charges the gas limit, not the amount used. `onReport` measured 22,880 declined
> and 66,764 settling.

### Privy ($5,000) —— 未开工，卡在 App ID

> ⚠️ 需要你去 <https://dashboard.privy.io> 注册并建一个 app，把 **App ID** 给我（约两分钟）。
> `PrivyProvider` 在挂载时就要这个值，没有它连启动都启动不了，所以这一步无法先写后配。

接入点已经具备，不需要重构：`lib/wallet.ts` 的 `injected()` 返回一个 EIP-1193 provider，
而 Privy 的嵌入式钱包同样暴露 EIP-1193（`useWallets()` → `getEthereumProvider()`），
所以 Privy 是在这个既有接缝上多加一个来源，`lib/signer.ts` 完全不用动。

打算这样申报（等能真跑起来再定稿）：

> Passkeys are the primary account path, but WebAuthn PRF does not exist in desktop Chrome's built-in
> authenticator — so the fallback cannot assume a browser extension either, or the people most likely
> to need it are the people it excludes. Privy's embedded wallet is that fallback: an email login
> produces a signer, and the same `Signer` interface carries it to the contract.
